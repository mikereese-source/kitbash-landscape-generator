# Cave entrances: references for M5 step 5.2 (2026-10-28)

Mike's bar for 5.2: **a cave entrance is somewhere you walk into.** It starts on the ground, at ground level,
and the rock closes over your head. The 5.1 test shape (a half-buried sphere) reads as a pit, which is the wrong
kind of opening. This sheet collects real entrances and what they have in common, so 5.2 is built against them
and judged by eye against the same list (D91: by eye first, data alongside).

## What a walk-in entrance is made of
1. **A rock face above it.** A walk-in entrance opens at the foot of a cliff, a scarp or a steep hillside, where
   there's enough rock over the opening to make a roof. Flat open ground can only give a pit or a collapse,
   never a walk-in. The roof over the mouth is usually at least as thick as the entrance is tall.
2. **A floor flush with the ground outside.** There's no step or lip: the floor carries on from the ground at the
   foot of the face. It's level or falls gently inward. It's often covered in fallen rock (breakdown).
3. **A low, wide arch.** Most entrances are wider than they are tall. In layered rock (Mesa, Canyon) the roof
   follows a bedding plane and is nearly flat. Lava tubes and water-worn tubes are rounded or elliptical.
4. **A drip line.** The lip of the roof overhangs. Fallen rock and talus collect under the drip line, outside
   the mouth (Hinds Cave site map: the talus line follows the overhang's drip line).
5. **Zones going in.** The entrance zone, then a twilight zone where light fades over a few entrance-widths,
   then dark (tunnel or cavern, layouts 2 and 3).
6. **Size.** The usual definition of a cave is at least 5 m long and humanly passable. A shelter is a
   horizontal recess under an overhang, enterable but shallow.

The width-to-height ratio in these runs from about 0.6:1 (sea cave) to 5:1 (Cathedral Caverns, alcoves).
A typical walk-in is about 1.5–2:1.

## What this means for 5.2 (proposed)
- **Placement:** put the mouth at the **foot of a steep face**: steep ground above the spot (from the slope
  mask) and gentle ground at it. It faces out from the face. Across and Front to Back pick roughly where; the
  tool snaps to the nearest face foot, and warns if the landscape has no face high enough for a roof.
- **Floor:** a flat floor cut at the ground height of the mouth, so the cave floor runs in flush with the ground
  outside, then falls gently inward (Descent, for tunnels).
- **Mouth shape:** a low arch, Entrance Width (meters) plus an **Entrance Height**, starting at about 2:1.
  A flatter roof on layered types (Mesa, Canyon, Badlands, Cliffs) and a rounder one on Volcanic.
- **Drip line and rubble:** the roof lip stays sharp and overhanging; a little fallen rock in front, later.
- **Going in:** Entrance = a shelter a few widths deep; Tunnel = it narrows and winds on; Cavern = it opens
  into a chamber.

**Takeaways:**
1. Real cave walls are built from **planes**: bedding planes (flat, layered) and joints (vertical). The pass-1
   cell pattern (Voronoi joints, scallop dishes) is what read as a turtle shell; it doesn't match any of these.
2. The look depends on the **rock**: layered limestone (beds, flat roof), jointed rock or sea cave (vertical
   planes), lava tube (smooth tube, flow ledges). That suggests a **Rock** choice on the cave.
3. Fallen rock is **heaped** under where it fell, biggest blocks lowest.
4. Small detail (scallops, flowstone texture, crystals) is for the M6 material, not the mesh.

## How I'd build it (proposal for Mike, D105; nothing built yet)
1. **Shell density first.** Keep working the shape out on the coarse grid (fast), then smoothly subdivide the
   shell to about 150k / 1M / 4M faces at Preview / High / Ultra, so it's as dense as the face budget allows.
   Wall detail is applied after that, at the fine surface, so it can't facet into a turtle shell.
2. **Walls from planes, by Rock** (a new choice on the cave):
   - **Limestone** (default): flat bedding planes across the whole cave, 0.5–2 m apart with varied thickness. Each
     bed steps the wall in or out a little and some get an undercut notch at their base. The roof snaps flat
     to the nearest bed above the passage. A long, gentle wobble keeps it from looking machined.
   - **Jointed** (sea caves, cliffs): a few long, straight vertical fracture planes per passage, each pushing the
     wall in or out as a flat face; the beds weaker. A smooth wave-worn band low down when it opens to water.
   - **Lava tube**: a smooth round section, only two or three long horizontal flow ledges, little break-up.
   - Roughness then adds broken rock on top, at sizes the face budget can show. The pass-1 cell pattern and
     dishes go.
3. **Rocks as separate instances**, never cut into the shell:
   - **Breakdown heaps**: fallen blocks piled where they'd fall, under the roof and at the wall foot, big blocks
     lowest, scattered smaller ones around. Rubble sets how many.
   - **Slabby rubble** under limestone beds, round boulders in lava tubes, angular blocks for jointed rock.
   - **Swappable**: each kind comes from a rock collection (KitBash rocks by default); swap in your own meshes
     for a game look. Made real when baked; instances in Unreal.
4. **Stalactites and stalagmites (pass 2)** the same way, as instances (with flowstone drapes from ledges).
5. **Floor detail (pass 3)**: the smooth sediment floor from the Mammoth passages, puddle hollows, slabs.
6. **Material detail (M6)**: scallops, flowstone and crystal texture as normal maps.

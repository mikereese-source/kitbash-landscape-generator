## Why
Mike, 2026-11-16: competitors don't ship Preview/High/Ultra geometry ("the geo is the geo"), and his scan references (Megascans, `D:\Megascans\VaultCache\FabLibrary`) read sharper and more realistic, especially hard cliffs, with fewer, well-placed polygons. Proposal: one mesh with a face budget between Preview and High, built to be sharp, with tertiary detail left to materials (D157, D163).
What we know now: each Detail Level samples the same fields on a finer grid (Preview 513 x 513, High 2049 x 2049 points). The erosion map is 512 cells at every level (LG-078), read with cubic interpolation, so big forms can't be sharper than about 8-12 m on a 2 km landscape; High's extra points mostly draw grain noise (D47) and the mesh-scale passes (LG-077 breakup, LG-086 crest lift). Softness comes from smooth fields, not from the face count. Scans look sharp because their forms are faceted (flat faces, hard creases) and their triangles sit where the curvature is.

## Done when
- Test 1, placement alone: today's 2 km Mountains at High (4.2M faces), decimated headless with Blender's Decimate (collapse) to about 1M and 500k faces, rendered in clay beside Preview and High from the same cameras (wide, mid on an inner peak, cliff close-up; neutral sky, low sun as c191/c194); crest sharpness, teeth and over 60 deg measured for each
- Test 2, grain out of the mesh: the same with the grain (D47) turned off in the geometry, to show how much tertiary detail materials would have to carry

- No add-on code changes; scratch in `docs/realism/LG-087_scratch/` on the branch (CODING.md), squash merge of the board and Log only

Context from the discussion: close-up hard cliffs need real geometry for their silhouette, so terrain plus Kit cliff meshes on steep zones stays in the picture; materials carry tertiary detail from scan textures, driven by baked masks (M7, LG-083).

(1) Face budget: about 1M triangles carries everything High has at wide and mid shot distance (every measure within 1% of High, teeth 47 -> 49, height error rms 0.42 m); 500k holds for wide shots only. So High's 8.4M triangles are about 8 times more than its forms need, and at Preview's own count (0.5M) well-placed triangles sit 3 to 4 times closer to High than the even grid does. (2) What Decimate fixes: weight, nothing else. No number moved and no picture got sharper. Soft forms stay soft: crest sharpness 1141 -> 1134, and up close the pillows are the same pillows. It also costs: about 85 s per mesh, long sliver triangles that shade dark on steep walls, and the regular grid is gone (per-point masks and attributes must be baked to textures, no tiling or sculpt-friendly topology, Preview no longer a subset of High). (3) What it can't do: the scan look. The Megascans Granite Cliff is 8 m wide and ships at 14,735 triangles with a normal map baked from 2.0M; that is hundreds of triangles per square meter of rock face against our 2 per square meter of ground at High. Its sharpness is flat planes meeting in creases, in the form and in the normal map. No terrain mesh at any count on this board has a flat face or a crease at 170 m. (4) Spike 2 (faceted generation) is worth doing, and is where the sharpness has to come from: test 1 shows triangle placement is not the lever, the fields are. Suggested order: faceted fields first (plane cells at cliff scale, creases, less blur on steep rock), judged on today's High grid; then one adaptive mesh at about 1M triangles as the export step (a mesher that follows curvature, not Blender's Decimate: no slivers, and it must port to three.js); close-up cliffs still need Kit cliff meshes or SDF, and tertiary detail the scan normal maps (M7). Test 2 (grain out of the mesh) not run. Not tested here: planar Decimate, flat or auto-smooth shading on the decimated mesh, scan meshes rendered in the same clay
